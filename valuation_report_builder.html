Forced Sale Definition
✓ Standard definition ✎ rename▼
A Forced Sale is one in which the assets are sold within a limited period of time, to one or more unconnected third parties with no special interest and does not take into consideration any element of Goodwill or Work in Progress. The physical assets are sold as a whole or individually and although sold without the benefit of any warranty or guarantee, are assumed to be in fair, working condition at the time of sale, unless otherwise stated. Such assets are sold wherever possible in situ.
✓ Assumptions list (desktop / list-based valuation) ✎ rename▼
A Forced Sale valuation is what we would expect to achieve from a well-advertised sale in situ. This valuation assumes: a willing seller; all machines are complete, well maintained and in full working order; sufficient time to prepare sales details, advertise and conduct a sale; easy access for removal; all items are wholly owned by the company; and the list provided is accurate, as we have not had the opportunity to inspect the equipment.
✓ Open Market — auction price basis (pre-pack / connected party review) ✎ rename▼
In this case, Open Market values are determined by estimated auction prices, if we were to arrange a well-advertised sale, or series of sales on site. For the purposes of this valuation, however, no account has been made of the cost of sale.
✓ By way of comparison — Forced Sale figure ✎ rename▼
By way of comparison, we would advise that the Forced Sale value of these assets would have been a figure in the region of £[X]. A forced sale assumes a disposal within a limited time period, in a private treaty sale or series of sales on site to trade dealers, or where most of the assets are removed from site for sale elsewhere.
